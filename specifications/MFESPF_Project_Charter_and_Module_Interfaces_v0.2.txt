======================================================================
1. PROJECT IDENTITY
======================================================================

1.1 Project Name

Modular Falsifiable Earth-System Physics Framework

Working abbreviation: MFESPF

Working descriptive title:

A computational laboratory for deriving, testing, and falsifying physical
explanations of Earth-system behavior and climate change.

1.2 Project Purpose

The purpose of MFESPF is to construct a modular computational representation
of the Earth system from established physical laws, measured physical
properties, and explicitly documented approximations.

The framework will be used to:

1. Test whether proposed physical mechanisms can reproduce observed
   Earth-system behavior.
2. Reconstruct historical climate states from declared initial and boundary
   conditions.
3. Identify characteristic timescales that emerge from coupled physical
   dynamics.
4. Separate necessary mechanisms from mechanisms that are unnecessary,
   inadequate, or observationally indistinguishable.
5. Produce falsifiable predictions with declared uncertainty.
6. Progress eventually to registered, out-of-sample prediction.

The framework is not intended to affirm or reject a predetermined explanation
of climate change. It shall provide a common physical and numerical laboratory
in which competing mechanisms can be implemented, isolated, perturbed,
measured, and compared with independent observations.

1.3 Central Scientific Question

Can a transparent model derived from physics reproduce major observed
Earth-system behavior and make successful predictions without allowing the
target observations to dictate its governing equations?

1.4 Project Motto

Every subsystem is optional.
Every approximation is visible.
Every claim is measurable.
Every prediction is falsifiable.
Every failure is reportable.

======================================================================
2. SCIENTIFIC OBJECTIVE AND SUCCESS LADDER
======================================================================

2.1 Primary Objective

Build a transparent physical model capable of reproducing observed
Earth-system behavior, identifying the physical mechanisms and timescales
necessary to explain that behavior, and eventually generating successful
out-of-sample predictions.

2.2 Scientific Success Levels

Level 1: Internal Physical Correctness

The model satisfies applicable conservation laws, dimensional checks,
limiting cases, analytic comparisons, numerical convergence requirements,
and interface accounting requirements.

Level 2: Process Reproduction

Individual modules reproduce independently measured physical processes within
a declared scope and uncertainty.

Level 3: Historical Reconstruction

The coupled framework reproduces selected historical observations when
provided with historically measured initial and boundary conditions.

Level 4: Cross-Observable Validation

One frozen physical configuration reproduces multiple independent observables
simultaneously. Agreement with one selected record is insufficient.

Level 5: Out-of-Sample Prediction

A frozen framework configuration successfully predicts observations that did
not determine its equations, parameters, initial state, or boundary
conditions.

2.3 Scientific Success Versus Project Success

The project may succeed scientifically even when a proposed mechanism fails.
A refuted mechanism, exposed numerical artifact, measured validity boundary,
or demonstrated inability to distinguish alternatives is a valid scientific
result if it is reproducible and correctly reported.

======================================================================
3. GOVERNING MODELING PHILOSOPHY
======================================================================

3.1 Governing Scientific Rule

No mechanism shall be added merely because it improves agreement with a
selected observational record. Every mechanism must have:

1. An independently defensible physical basis.
2. An explicit mathematical representation.
3. A declared validity domain.
4. Declared assumptions and approximations.
5. At least one limiting-case or correctness test.
6. At least one test capable of demonstrating its failure.

3.2 Physics Before Curve Fit

The required scientific sequence is:

Physical hypothesis
    -> governing equations
    -> derived prediction
    -> numerical implementation
    -> correctness tests
    -> comparison with withheld observation
    -> supported, qualified, refuted, or unresolved result

The prohibited sequence is:

Target observation
    -> arbitrary fitted equation
    -> agreement with same observation
    -> claim of physical validation

3.3 Cross-Observable Requirement

A model that fits surface temperature while failing the associated energy
budget, atmospheric structure, spectral radiation, ocean heat uptake, or
other applicable independent observables shall not be described as physically
validated.

3.4 Narrow-Claim Rule

Every conclusion shall be no broader than the tested configuration, variable
range, spatial domain, temporal domain, spectral resolution, numerical
resolution, and uncertainty permit.

3.5 Negative-Result Rule

Failed mechanisms, unstable formulations, null results, finite-domain
artifacts, parameter degeneracies, and abandoned equations shall be retained
in the experiment record when they affect scientific interpretation.

4. FORMAL DEFINITIONS
======================================================================

The following definitions are normative for MFESPF documentation and software.

4.1 Framework

The complete software, mathematical, data, test, and reporting environment
that permits Earth-system modules to be implemented and coupled.

4.2 Architecture

The set of permitted module roles, interfaces, data contracts, lifecycle
rules, conservation rules, configuration rules, and dependency boundaries.
Architecture defines what may be represented without requiring that every
representation be active in every run.

4.3 Architectural Carve-Out

A reserved and documented place in the architecture for a potentially
relevant physical domain. A carve-out guarantees that an implementation may
be added through defined interfaces. It does not assert that the domain is
important, necessary, or active.

4.4 Executable Configuration

A complete declaration of the modules, implementations, equations, input
data, parameters, resolutions, solvers, tolerances, and couplings active in a
specific run.

4.5 Module

A replaceable computational unit that owns a defined portion of the model
state, advances or diagnoses that state, and interacts with other modules only
through approved interfaces.

4.6 Module Implementation

A specific fidelity-level realization of a module contract. Multiple
implementations may satisfy the same contract, such as a gray-radiation model,
a banded radiation model, and a line-by-line radiation model.

4.7 Coupler

A component responsible for scheduled exchanges among modules. A coupler may
convert grids, aggregate time steps, reconcile units, enforce flux direction,
and verify exchange conservation. It shall not silently add physical behavior.

4.8 State Variable

A quantity owned by one module whose value evolves in time or simulation step
according to governing equations. Examples include temperature, water-vapor
mixing ratio, cloud-water mass, ocean heat content, or ice fraction.

4.9 Diagnostic Variable

A quantity calculated from state variables and inputs but not independently
evolved. Examples include outgoing longwave radiation, radiative imbalance,
or relative humidity when derived from temperature, pressure, and water vapor.

4.10 Prognostic Variable

A state variable explicitly advanced through time by an evolution equation.

4.11 Prescribed Variable

A quantity supplied from a dataset, experiment definition, or external module
rather than evolved by the receiving module.

4.12 Control Variable

A quantity deliberately fixed or manipulated to isolate the effect of another
quantity in a controlled experiment.

4.13 Parameter

A named quantity used by an equation but not evolved as state during a given
run. Each parameter must be classified as fundamental, measured, derived,
parameterized, or calibrated.

4.14 Fundamental Quantity

A physical constant or universal relationship accepted as an input to the
framework at a declared precision and provenance.

4.15 Measured Quantity

A property obtained independently through observation or laboratory
measurement and supplied to the framework with units, provenance, and
measurement uncertainty.

4.16 Derived Quantity

A quantity calculated from governing equations and other declared quantities.

4.17 Constitutive Relationship

A relationship describing how a particular material or phase responds, such
as an equation of state, viscosity law, absorption coefficient, or heat
capacity relation.

4.18 Parameterization

An explicit reduced representation of unresolved or intentionally omitted
physics. A parameterization shall identify the higher-fidelity process it
approximates and the expected validity range.

4.19 Calibration

The estimation or adjustment of one or more parameters using observational or
experimental data. Calibration shall be declared and shall not be represented
as independent validation.

4.20 Initial Condition

The declared system state at the starting time of an evolution.

4.21 Boundary Condition

A declared physical condition at the spatial, material, or system boundary,
including incoming or outgoing energy, matter, momentum, radiation, or charge.

4.22 Forcing

A prescribed influence on a module or coupled system. A forcing may vary in
space, time, direction, or spectrum and must be represented through a declared
interface.

4.23 Flux

The rate at which an extensive quantity crosses a defined interface or unit
area. Every flux shall declare quantity, units, direction, sign convention,
time interval, and spatial support.

4.24 Source and Sink

A process that produces or removes a tracked quantity inside a module. Sources
and sinks must be physically identified and included in conservation
accounting.

4.25 Conservation Residual

The difference between the change in stored quantity and the integrated net
transfer plus internal sources and sinks over an accounting interval.

For a conserved quantity Q:

R_Q = Delta Q_stored - Integral(S_Q - L_Q + F_Q,in - F_Q,out) dt

where S_Q is an internal source and L_Q is an internal loss. If Q is strictly
conserved within the modeled boundary, allowable physical sources and losses
must sum consistently across the larger system.

4.26 Physical Validity Domain

The declared conditions under which an equation or implementation is expected
to represent the intended process, including ranges of temperature, pressure,
composition, spatial scale, temporal scale, and spectral resolution.

4.27 Numerical Validity Domain

The range of time steps, grid sizes, tolerances, solver settings, and state
values for which numerical correctness gates have been passed.

4.28 Fidelity Level

A documented degree of physical and numerical detail for a module. Fidelity
level is module-specific and does not imply universal superiority.

4.29 Historical Replay

A run in which one or more measured histories are prescribed as initial or
boundary conditions and model outputs are compared with historical
observations.

4.30 Hindcast

A run configured using only information declared available before a cutoff,
then evaluated against later observations.

4.31 Out-of-Sample Prediction

A prediction generated from a frozen model before the evaluated observations
are introduced into model construction, parameter selection, or run setup.

4.32 Falsifiable Claim

A precise statement that identifies a mechanism, expected measurable result,
uncertainty method, applicable scope, and an observation or experimental
outcome that would count against the claim.

4.33 Supported Within Scope

A claim status indicating that the declared tests were passed within the
specified validity domain and uncertainty. It does not imply universal proof.

4.34 Qualified

A claim status indicating partial support, restricted validity, material
caveats, inconsistent observables, or sensitivity that prevents a broader
conclusion.

4.35 Refuted

A claim status indicating that a predefined failure condition was met and the
failure cannot be explained by a known invalid numerical result.

4.36 Unresolved

A claim status indicating that available numerical precision, observations,
uncertainty, or identifiability is inadequate to support or refute the claim.

4.37 Structural Uncertainty

Uncertainty resulting from the selected mathematical representation,
omitted physics, module structure, or alternative physically plausible
formulations.

4.38 Numerical Error

Difference introduced by discretization, finite precision, iterative solver
tolerance, interpolation, operator splitting, or other computational methods.
Numerical error is not the same as physical uncertainty.

4.39 Predictive Interval

A declared interval intended to contain a future or withheld observation at a
specified coverage level under the stated model and uncertainty assumptions.

4.40 Physics Manifest

The human-readable and machine-readable declaration of which modules and
physical processes are active, inactive, prescribed, or replaced in a run.

6. MODULE ARCHITECTURE STANDARD
======================================================================

6.1 Module Independence

A module shall own its internal state. Another module shall not directly alter
that state. Exchanges shall occur through typed interface messages or coupler-
managed shared fields with documented ownership.

6.2 Required Module Contract

Every module implementation shall provide:

- module_id
- module_name
- module_version
- interface_version
- fidelity_level
- physical_basis
- governing_equations
- assumptions
- validity_domain
- owned_state_variables
- required_inputs
- produced_outputs
- exported_fluxes
- imported_fluxes
- conserved_quantities
- internal_sources_and_sinks
- supported_spatial_representations
- supported_temporal_representations
- supported_spectral_representations, if applicable
- initialization_method
- advance_method
- diagnostic_method
- checkpoint_method
- restore_method
- finalize_method
- uncertainty_outputs
- correctness_tests
- known_limitations
- expected_resource_class

6.3 Standard Module Lifecycle

The framework shall call modules through the following conceptual lifecycle:

1. register()
2. configure(configuration)
3. validate_configuration()
4. initialize(initial_state, boundary_state)
5. expose_capabilities()
6. negotiate_interfaces()
7. begin_step(time, dt)
8. receive_inputs(interface_packets)
9. compute_tendencies()
10. advance_state(dt)
11. emit_outputs()
12. report_conservation()
13. report_uncertainty()
14. checkpoint(), when requested
15. finalize()

A particular programming language may use different function names, but it
shall preserve the lifecycle semantics.

6.4 Module Status

Each module in a run shall have exactly one primary status:

ACTIVE
    The module evolves owned state and participates in coupling.

PRESCRIBED
    The module supplies data from a declared dataset or function but does not
    evolve the corresponding state internally.

DIAGNOSTIC
    The module consumes data and computes outputs without changing the
    evolution.

INACTIVE
    The module does not participate in the run.

STUB
    The module satisfies an interface for integration testing but has no claim
    to physical fidelity. Stub outputs must be visibly marked.

6.5 Module Dependency Rule

Dependencies shall be declared explicitly. Circular dependencies must be
resolved through a coupler or iterative coupling schedule. Hidden dependencies
on global mutable state are prohibited.

6.6 Replaceability Rule

Two implementations may be substituted when they satisfy the same versioned
interface contract. Any changed assumption, output meaning, resolution, or
conservation behavior must be declared in the run manifest.

7. COMMON DATA MODEL
======================================================================

7.1 Standard Field Metadata

Every exchanged field shall include:

- field_id
- physical_name
- symbol
- quantity_kind
- units
- data_type
- dimensions
- coordinate_system
- spatial_support
- time_stamp
- time_interval
- averaging_method
- spectral_coordinate, if applicable
- angular_coordinate, if applicable
- phase_or_species, if applicable
- sign_convention
- missing_value_policy
- uncertainty_representation
- provenance
- quality_flags

7.2 Quantity Kinds

The framework shall distinguish at least:

- intensive state
- extensive state
- flux
- source rate
- sink rate
- tendency
- boundary value
- diagnostic
- probability distribution
- uncertainty field
- categorical state

7.3 Units

Internal interfaces shall use a declared coherent unit system, preferably SI.
Conversions shall occur at controlled boundaries and be logged. Fields with
incompatible dimensions shall fail validation rather than be silently coerced.

7.4 Coordinates

A field shall identify its coordinate system and location convention. Examples
include:

- global scalar
- atmospheric column
- pressure level
- altitude level
- latitude band
- spherical surface grid
- Cartesian test domain
- irregular mesh
- ocean depth layer
- spectral wavelength grid
- spectral wavenumber grid

7.5 Temporal Semantics

A field shall explicitly state whether it is:

- instantaneous
- average over interval
- accumulation over interval
- start-of-step state
- midpoint state
- end-of-step state
- predictor state
- corrected state

7.6 Uncertainty Payload

Where uncertainty exists, a field may carry:

- standard uncertainty
- covariance reference
- lower and upper bounds
- ensemble member coordinate
- probability distribution identifier
- quality classification
- unresolved uncertainty flag

The representation must declare its assumptions.

======================================================================
8. CONSERVED-QUANTITY INTERFACE
======================================================================

8.1 Conserved Quantities

Where applicable, the framework shall track:

- total energy
- mass
- water mass
- momentum
- angular momentum
- electric charge
- elemental inventories
- chemical species inventories

8.2 Flux Sign Convention

Each interface shall define one positive direction. The recommended convention
is positive into the receiving module. The sending module shall report the
same transfer with equal magnitude and opposite sign in its own budget.

8.3 Transfer Transaction

A coupled transfer shall include:

- transfer_id
- sender_module
- receiver_module
- conserved_quantity
- amount_or_flux
- units
- start_time
- end_time
- spatial_mapping
- numerical_transfer_error
- physical_source_or_sink_reference

8.4 Conservation Reconciliation

At the end of each coupling interval, the coupler shall compare exported and
imported transfers. Any mismatch above tolerance shall be recorded as a
coupling residual.

8.5 No Double Counting

A physical transfer may be represented exactly once in the coupled budget.
For example, latent heat carried from surface evaporation into the atmosphere
must not also be independently added as an atmospheric heat source without a
matching surface removal.

8.6 Stabilization Accounting

Numerical filters, clipping, diffusion, limiters, and stabilization methods
that alter a conserved quantity shall report that alteration as a separately
identified numerical source or sink.

9. CORE MODULE INTERFACES
======================================================================

The interfaces below are architectural contracts. The first implementation
may use only a subset of their optional fields.

9.1 Solar Source Module

Purpose:
Represent energy and particle outputs originating from the Sun at a declared
reference surface.

Required inputs:
- simulation time
- requested spectral grid
- reference distance
- selected solar model or dataset

Required outputs:
- total solar irradiance
- spectral solar irradiance
- output reference distance
- irradiance uncertainty
- solar-state metadata

Optional outputs:
- angular distribution
- polarization
- solar energetic particle flux
- magnetic-field descriptors

Conservation role:
Provides external energy entering the Earth-system boundary. It does not
itself add absorbed energy to Earth modules.

Minimum implementations:
- constant measured spectrum
- time-varying measured total irradiance with fixed normalized spectrum
- time-varying measured spectrum
- physically evolving solar source

9.2 Orbital Geometry Module

Purpose:
Calculate Earth-Sun geometry and map solar-source output to location, time,
and orientation.

Required inputs:
- simulation time
- orbital parameters or ephemeris
- Earth shape and rotation parameters
- solar source reference distance

Required outputs:
- Earth-Sun distance
- solar zenith geometry
- day length
- local incident-angle factor
- top-of-atmosphere irradiance before atmospheric interaction

Optional outputs:
- tidal potential
- lunar geometry
- planetary perturbation diagnostics

Correctness gates:
- inverse-square distance behavior
- spherical-area integration
- symmetric limiting configurations
- energy consistency between intercepted disk and distributed surface flux

9.3 Spectral Radiation Module

Purpose:
Transport shortwave and longwave radiation through media and across module
boundaries.

Required inputs:
- incident spectral radiance or irradiance
- temperature fields
- pressure fields
- material composition
- optical properties
- layer or spatial geometry
- surface spectral properties

Required outputs:
- upward and downward spectral flux
- absorbed energy by layer or material
- emitted energy by layer or material
- reflected and transmitted energy
- top-of-atmosphere flux
- surface radiative flux
- spectral heating rates

Spectral contract:
The module shall not assume an immutable spectral distribution. It must accept
an externally supplied grid or negotiate a compatible spectral representation.

Supported spectral representations may include:
- broadband
- fixed bands
- correlated-k or equivalent reduced bands
- adaptive bands
- high-resolution spectrum
- line-by-line spectrum

Conservation role:
Incident energy must equal absorbed plus reflected plus transmitted energy
within numerical tolerance, accounting for internal thermal emission.

Required uncertainty outputs:
- spectral discretization error estimate
- optical-property uncertainty contribution
- solver residual

9.4 Atmospheric State and Dynamics Module

Purpose:
Own atmospheric thermodynamic and dynamic state and evolve it under resolved
and parameterized forces.

Owned state may include:
- pressure
- density
- temperature
- velocity
- composition
- water species
- atmospheric energy

Required inputs:
- radiative heating rates
- surface momentum, sensible heat, latent heat, and mass fluxes
- gravity
- chemical tendencies
- cloud and aerosol tendencies where separately owned
- boundary conditions

Required outputs:
- updated atmospheric state
- pressure and density profile
- temperature profile
- winds or reduced transport variables
- advective fluxes
- convective tendencies
- atmospheric conservation budget

Permitted fidelity path:
- hydrostatic column
- radiative-convective column
- latitude-resolved reduced dynamics
- two-dimensional dynamics
- three-dimensional primitive equations
- nonhydrostatic dynamics where justified

9.5 Atmospheric Chemistry Module

Purpose:
Evolve chemical species and reaction pathways without owning bulk atmospheric
motion.

Required inputs:
- species concentrations
- temperature and pressure
- actinic or spectral radiation
- transport tendencies
- emissions and deposition fluxes
- aerosol surface information where applicable

Required outputs:
- species tendencies
- chemical heating or cooling
- photochemical products
- deposition and transformation rates
- elemental conservation report

Interface rule:
The chemistry module may request radiation at chemically relevant wavelengths,
but it shall not silently redefine the radiation module's spectrum.

9.6 Water Phase and Hydrology Module

Purpose:
Track water among vapor, liquid, solid, surface, soil, and transport stores.

Owned state may include:
- atmospheric water vapor
- cloud liquid and ice, if not cloud-owned
- rain and snow stores
- soil moisture
- surface water
- groundwater

Required inputs:
- temperature and pressure
- surface energy state
- wind or exchange parameters
- precipitation
- evaporation and sublimation potentials
- transport fields

Required outputs:
- water phase-change tendencies
- evaporation and sublimation fluxes
- runoff
- infiltration
- water-storage changes
- latent-energy transfers
- total water budget

Conservation rule:
Every phase transfer must conserve total water and exchange latent energy with
the applicable receiving and sending modules.

9.7 Cloud Module

Purpose:
Represent cloud state, formation, evolution, precipitation coupling, and
radiative properties as a distinct variable set.

Owned state may include:
- cloud fraction
- liquid water content
- ice water content
- droplet size distribution
- crystal size distribution
- cloud altitude and thickness
- cloud optical depth
- cloud lifetime variables

Required inputs:
- atmospheric temperature and pressure
- water-vapor state
- vertical motion or convective tendency
- aerosol or nuclei information when implemented
- spectral radiation
- hydrological phase-transfer data

Required outputs:
- cloud-state tendencies
- shortwave optical properties
- longwave optical properties
- phase-transition mass transfers
- precipitation source terms
- cloud radiative diagnostics
- cloud uncertainty contribution

Fidelity path:
- no-cloud control
- prescribed static cloud state
- prescribed time-varying cloud state
- reduced dynamic cloud equations
- bulk microphysics
- detailed microphysics
- particle-resolved implementation

Cloud reporting rule:
Shortwave and longwave effects shall be reported separately before any net
cloud radiative effect is reported.

9.8 Aerosol Module

Purpose:
Track aerosol state and expose optical, chemical, and cloud-nucleation effects.

Required inputs:
- emissions or source terms
- atmospheric transport state
- humidity
- temperature
- chemical tendencies
- deposition conditions

Required outputs:
- aerosol mass and number fields
- size distribution
- spectral optical properties
- deposition fluxes
- cloud-nuclei properties where implemented
- aerosol conservation budget

9.9 Land-Surface Module

Purpose:
Own land thermal, radiative, moisture, and material state.

Required inputs:
- incident shortwave and longwave radiation
- atmospheric temperature, humidity, wind, and pressure
- precipitation
- hydrological state
- land-cover and material properties

Required outputs:
- reflected shortwave spectrum
- emitted longwave spectrum
- sensible heat flux
- latent heat flux
- ground heat flux
- surface momentum flux
- surface temperature
- surface-water transfers
- land energy and water budgets

9.10 Ocean Module

Purpose:
Own ocean thermal, mechanical, salinity, and chemical state at the selected
fidelity.

Required inputs:
- surface radiative flux
- sensible and latent heat exchange
- freshwater flux
- momentum flux
- river input
- ice exchange
- gas or chemical exchange

Required outputs:
- sea-surface temperature
- ocean heat uptake
- stored heat
- surface salinity
- return heat, moisture, and momentum fluxes
- circulation or reduced transport diagnostics
- ocean energy, water, and salt budgets

Fidelity path:
- fixed boundary temperature
- single thermal reservoir
- mixed-layer and deep-ocean reservoirs
- latitude-resolved transport
- reduced circulation
- three-dimensional dynamic ocean

9.11 Cryosphere Module

Purpose:
Represent snow, sea ice, glaciers, ice sheets, and permafrost.

Required inputs:
- atmospheric and ocean temperature
- precipitation
- radiative flux
- surface and ocean heat flux
- mechanical stresses where implemented

Required outputs:
- ice and snow state
- phase-change energy transfer
- freshwater transfer
- surface spectral properties
- insulation effects
- ice dynamics where implemented
- cryosphere mass and energy budgets

9.12 Biosphere Module

Purpose:
Represent biological processes that exchange energy, water, gases, nutrients,
and surface properties with other modules.

Required inputs:
- radiation
- temperature
- water availability
- atmospheric and soil composition
- nutrient state
- land or ocean habitat state

Required outputs:
- gas exchange
- transpiration
- biological heat or energy terms where significant
- biomass and carbon changes
- surface-property changes
- biological aerosol or chemical source terms

9.13 Biogeochemistry Module

Purpose:
Track elemental and chemical reservoirs and transfers across atmosphere,
ocean, land, biosphere, and solid Earth.

Required outputs include separate budgets for each modeled conserved element.

9.14 Solid-Earth Module

Purpose:
Represent geothermal, volcanic, tectonic, weathering, and long-term boundary
changes at appropriate timescales.

The module may operate as prescribed history, event source, reduced dynamic
model, or higher-fidelity physical model.

9.15 Gravity Module

Purpose:
Provide gravitational acceleration, potential, and tidal terms required by
other modules.

Fidelity path:
- constant-g approximation
- altitude- and latitude-dependent Newtonian gravity
- spatial gravitational field
- relativistic correction where a defined claim requires it

9.16 Particle Interaction Module

Purpose:
Represent charged-particle, ionization, collision, cascade, nuclear, isotope,
or particle-induced chemical processes when required by a falsifiable claim.

The module shall remain inactive unless a run identifies the mechanism, input
particle environment, target observables, and failure criterion.

9.17 Quantum and Molecular Properties Module

Purpose:
Supply quantum-derived or externally calculated atomic and molecular
properties, including transition frequencies, line strengths, partition
functions, scattering properties, and reaction information.

The initial framework may use measured or curated molecular properties.
Explicit quantum calculation may replace those inputs through the same
interface when scientifically justified.

9.18 Human Activity Module

Purpose:
Translate documented human activities into explicit physical changes, such as
species emissions, aerosol sources, land-state changes, water transfers, or
energy fluxes.

The module shall not directly alter diagnostic climate outputs. It shall act
through ordinary physical interfaces.

======================================================================
10. SPECTRAL INTERFACE STANDARD
======================================================================

10.1 General Representation

Spectral quantities shall be expressible as:

X = X(s, x, t, d, p)

where:
- s is wavelength, frequency, wavenumber, energy, or band identifier;
- x is position;
- t is time;
- d is direction or angular coordinate when required;
- p represents polarization or other spectral-state metadata when required.

10.2 Spectral Grid Descriptor

Each spectral packet shall declare:

- spectral_coordinate_type
- coordinate_units
- bin_edges or sample_coordinates
- bin_widths
- integration_convention
- spectral_density_units
- ordering
- interpolation_policy
- extrapolation_policy
- resolution_identifier

10.3 Spectral Regridding

Spectral regridding shall conserve integrated energy to a declared tolerance.
The regridding method and residual shall be logged.

10.4 Dynamic Spectra

The framework shall permit spectra to vary with:

- time
- solar state
- temperature
- pressure
- molecular composition
- cloud state
- aerosol state
- surface state
- viewing or propagation direction

10.5 Spectral Fidelity Comparison

A lower-fidelity spectral module shall be testable against a higher-fidelity
implementation over the lower-fidelity module's declared validity domain.

======================================================================
11. TEMPORAL COUPLING AND EMERGENT TIMESCALES
======================================================================

11.1 Time Is a Model Coordinate, Not a Fixed Climate Index

No single time index shall be assumed to characterize the entire Earth
system. Each module may have characteristic timescales derived from its
storage, fluxes, feedbacks, and governing equations.

11.2 Multirate Coupling

Modules may advance at different time steps. The coupler shall declare:

- master time coordinate
- module substeps
- exchange frequency
- interpolation method
- accumulation method
- synchronization points
- splitting order
- coupling convergence criterion

11.3 Timescale Experiments

The framework shall support:

- step response
- impulse response
- pulse response
- periodic forcing
- relaxation analysis
- eigenmode analysis
- lag and cross-correlation analysis
- transfer-function analysis
- frequency-dependent gain and phase

11.4 Historical Time Inputs

Historical datasets may be supplied at native or transformed intervals. Any
resampling shall declare aggregation, interpolation, uncertainty, and lost
frequency content.

======================================================================
12. SPATIAL REPRESENTATION AND REGRIDDING
======================================================================

12.1 Supported Spatial Abstractions

The architecture shall permit:

- zero-dimensional global mean
- one-dimensional vertical column
- one-dimensional latitude bands
- two-dimensional surface grids
- three-dimensional atmosphere or ocean grids
- irregular meshes
- regional nested domains

12.2 Spatial Exchange

Each field exchange shall identify whether a value is point-based, cell-
averaged, face-centered, layer-averaged, or integrated over a domain.

12.3 Conservative Regridding

Regridding of extensive quantities and fluxes shall conserve the integrated
quantity to a declared tolerance. Intensive quantities shall use methods
appropriate to their physical meaning.

12.4 Resolution Claims

No result shall claim spatial detail finer than supported by the active
physics, input data, numerical grid, and validation observations.

======================================================================
13. UNCERTAINTY INTERFACE
======================================================================

13.1 Required Uncertainty Categories

Modules shall report applicable contributions from:

- measurement uncertainty
- initial-condition uncertainty
- boundary-condition uncertainty
- parameter uncertainty
- constitutive-property uncertainty
- numerical discretization error
- iterative solver error
- structural uncertainty
- missing-physics uncertainty
- stochastic variability
- spatial-resolution uncertainty
- temporal-resolution uncertainty
- spectral-resolution uncertainty

13.2 Separation of Error Types

Numerical uncertainty, model uncertainty, and predictive uncertainty shall not
be merged without retaining their separate components.

13.3 Module Uncertainty Contract

A module shall identify:

- uncertain inputs
- uncertain parameters
- output quantities affected
- propagation method
- distributional assumptions
- correlations known or assumed
- unresolved contributions

13.4 Accepted Propagation Methods

Depending on assumptions and cost, methods may include:

- analytic propagation
- local sensitivity derivatives
- parameter sweeps
- Monte Carlo ensembles
- bounded intervals
- interval arithmetic
- Bayesian propagation
- model-form ensembles
- worst-case bounds

No method shall be used without documenting its assumptions.

======================================================================
15. FAILURE CRITERIA AND CORRECTNESS GATES
======================================================================

15.1 Mathematical Failure

- dimensional inconsistency
- ill-posed equations
- undefined state
- unphysical singularity
- incorrect limiting behavior
- required symmetry violation

15.2 Numerical Failure

- lack of time-step convergence
- lack of spatial convergence
- lack of spectral convergence
- domain-size artifact
- boundary contamination
- solver-dependent conclusion
- excess conservation residual
- impossible state created by numerics
- restart or reproducibility failure

15.3 Interface and Coupling Failure

- unmatched transfer
- incompatible units
- inconsistent sign convention
- double counting
- missing reciprocal exchange
- order-dependent result beyond tolerance
- interpolation or aggregation error sufficient to change a claim

15.4 Physical-Process Failure

- wrong response sign
- wrong equilibrium behavior
- wrong temporal response
- wrong spatial response
- wrong spectral response
- correct aggregate result from an incorrect budget

15.5 Empirical Failure

- withheld observation outside the declared predictive interval
- persistent directional bias beyond the declared tolerance
- inability to reproduce an independently measured process
- physically implausible parameter requirements
- failure of cross-observable consistency

15.6 Identifiability Failure

If two or more mechanisms cannot be distinguished within the available
observations and uncertainty, the result shall be UNABLE_TO_DISTINGUISH.

15.7 Minimum Correctness Gates

Each applicable module shall pass:

- dimensional analysis
- analytic or manufactured-solution test
- limiting-case test
- conservation test
- time-step convergence
- spatial convergence, when spatial
- spectral convergence, when spectral
- boundary sensitivity
- independent implementation or solver comparison where practical
- null or disabled-coupling control

20. DEVELOPMENT METHOD
======================================================================

For each new module, equation, or coupling:

1. Define the physical claim.
2. Identify the governing physics.
3. Derive or document the approximation.
4. Define the validity domain.
5. Define the module interface.
6. Implement the simplest valid form.
7. Verify units and limiting cases.
8. Verify conservation.
9. Verify numerical convergence.
10. Compare with an independent process-level observation.
11. Couple it to the framework.
12. Re-run conservation and convergence tests.
13. Run a null or disabled-coupling control.
14. Quantify uncertainty and sensitivity.
15. Record the claim status.
16. Preserve negative and qualified results.

======================================================================
23. EXPLICIT NON-ASSUMPTIONS
======================================================================

The project does not begin by assuming that:

- any one mechanism explains observed climate change;
- existing explanations are either correct or incorrect;
- solar variability dominates Earth-system behavior;
- atmospheric carbon dioxide dominates every timescale or observable;
- water vapor is exclusively a forcing or exclusively a feedback;
- clouds have one fixed net effect;
- particle physics is necessary for ordinary climate behavior;
- explicit quantum simulation is required at every scale;
- historical correlation establishes causation;
- a successful curve fit validates a mechanism;
- a failed mechanism invalidates all related physics;
- maximum resolution implies maximum scientific validity;
- greater complexity necessarily produces a better model.

These are questions to be tested through explicit claims and experiments.

======================================================================
24. CHARTER STATEMENT
======================================================================

MFESPF shall construct a computational representation of the Earth system
from explicit physical laws, measured properties, and documented
approximations.

The architecture shall preserve a place for every physical domain that may
become necessary, including solar physics, orbital mechanics, gravity,
radiation, atmospheric dynamics, chemistry, water, clouds, aerosols, oceans,
ice, land, biology, solid-Earth processes, particle physics, quantum physics,
external astrophysical influences, and human activity.

No executable configuration is required to activate every domain. Each run
shall state exactly which physics is active, prescribed, diagnostic, inactive,
or represented by a stub.

The framework shall support changing solar and terrestrial spectra, modular
fidelity, multiple spatial and temporal representations, historical replay,
controlled intervention, uncertainty propagation, and out-of-sample
prediction.

The framework shall be designed so that scientifically useful configurations
and the baseline correctness suite can run without a supercomputer. Higher-
fidelity modules may be substituted when justified by a defined claim.

Observational records shall not dictate governing equations. They may provide
measured properties, initial conditions, boundary conditions, calibration
where explicitly declared, and independent opportunities to falsify the
model's claims.

Every scientific conclusion shall be constrained by conservation laws,
numerical correctness, process-level validation, uncertainty, and a
predefined failure criterion.

The final authority shall not be the model, its author, an institution, or a
consensus position. The final authority shall be the degree to which explicit
physical predictions survive reproducible testing against reality.
